## OQ-0001 — Is the core contribution already published?
**Priority:** P0 · **Status:** IN PROGRESS · **Raised:** 2026-08-07

SPEC-1 §4's related-work matrix cites Park et al. 2023, AutoGen/MetaGPT, TruthfulQA,
and classical SIR. That is a 2023-era view of the field. The 2024–2026 literature on
multi-agent LLM safety, manipulated-knowledge propagation, and network-topology
effects is not represented at all. Before any engineering, we need to know whether
"misinformation spreads through LLM agent networks and topology/heterogeneity
modulates it" is already a published result.

**Blocking on:** deep literature sweep (running). Feeds
`docs/02-literature/PRIOR-ART-REVIEW.md`.
**Closes with:** a written positioning statement per SOP-020 §6.

framing. The generic contribution is published at least twice in peer-reviewed venues

## OQ-0002 — The logit probe measures the wrong model in the heterogeneous condition
**Priority:** P0 · **Status:** OPEN · **Raised:** 2026-08-07 · **Source:** SPEC-2 §3.5

`modal_prober.py` hardcodes `LLM(model="meta-llama/Meta-Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct")` and
applies it to *all* agent contexts. In the heterogeneous condition, agents are Qwen-2.5,
Gemini-2.5-Flash and Cerebras-served Llama-3. Probing their contexts with Llama-3.1-8B
does not measure those agents' beliefs — it measures what Llama-3.1-8B would believe
after reading their conversation.

This is not a detail. `B_i^(t)` is the dependent variable for every hypothesis, and H1
— the central claim — is precisely the comparison between the heterogeneous and
homogeneous conditions. If belief in one condition is measured on the agents' own
models and in the other is measured on a substitute model, **the primary comparison is
confounded with the measurement instrument.**

Compounding it: Gemini via the AI Studio API does not expose the token-level
logprobs this probe requires in the form assumed, and provider logprob support varies.
So the logit half of the dual probe is not uniformly computable.

**Options to evaluate:**
(a) Self-host every agent model on Modal/vLLM so logprobs are always available — raises cost, kills the "free API" premise.
(b) Drop the logit component; use verbal/paraphrase-consistency probing only, uniformly across all agents. Cheaper, uniform, weaker per-item resolution — mitigate with more paraphrase templates.
(c) Restrict the logit probe to conditions where it is available and report the two probe types as separate, separately-analysed measures, never mixed into one α-weighted scalar across conditions.
(d) Restrict the whole study to open-weight models that can be self-hosted.

**Closes with:** DR + `docs/03-design/CONSTRUCT-VALIDITY-BELIEF-METRIC.md`.

**Update 2026-08-07 (round 1).** A strong option (e) has appeared, and it is better than any
of (a)–(d): **Chuang et al., *Simulating Opinion Dynamics with Networks of LLM-based
Agents*, Findings of NAACL 2024 (arXiv:2311.09618).** The agent emits a free-text verbal
report; a *separate* classifier model (FLAN-T5-XXL) maps it to a numeric opinion scale. It is
peer-reviewed, human-validated ("no systematic bias" against human ratings), applies
identically to open-weight and closed-API agents, and the authors report it is **more
reliable than agents' self-reported belief ratings**. Adopting it removes the wrong-model
confound, makes the instrument uniform across conditions, and deletes the Modal/vLLM
dependency entirely — which also removes most of `OQ-0014`'s budget risk. Caveat: their
construct is a bipolar attitude scale, not a binary factual credence, so it is a template
requiring adaptation and its own validation. Round-2 sweep is checking the logit-calibration
and cross-model-probing literature before this is decided.

## OQ-0003 — Primary metrics are never formally defined
**Priority:** P0 · **Status:** OPEN · **Raised:** 2026-08-07 · **Source:** SPEC-1 §3, SPEC-2 §4

The hypotheses are stated in terms of `I∞` (H1), `BPI(T)` (H2), and "Truth Retention
Rate" (H4). SPEC-2's JSON schema emits `final_trr`, `final_mp`, `final_bpi`. SPEC-4
mentions "R₀ curves". **None of TRR, MP, BPI, I∞, or R₀ has a formula anywhere in the
four documents.** SPEC-1 §2 formalises only H(Θ), M_φ and B_i.

Every one of these involves a non-obvious choice. Is TRR the mean belief in the true
claim, or the fraction of agents above a threshold? What threshold? Is an agent
"infected" at B < 0.5, or does it require sustained belief? Is BPI a variance, a
bimodality coefficient, or a cluster count? Is R₀ estimated from early-round secondary
infections, and how, given that agents are not discretely infected? These choices
change the results.

**Closes with:** a formal metrics section in the preregistration, with each metric's
formula, threshold choices justified in advance, and a sensitivity analysis over the
thresholds.

## OQ-0004 — H4 has no corresponding experimental factor
**Priority:** P0 · **Status:** OPEN · **Raised:** 2026-08-07 · **Source:** SPEC-1 §3 vs SPEC-3 §1

H4 posits a critical seeding density ρ\* above which truth retention collapses. Testing
it requires **varying seeding density** — that is the independent variable.

The factorial matrix is `3 topologies × 3 populations × 15 facts × 3 seeds`. Seeding
density is not a factor. SPEC-2's example manifest fixes `seed_agent_ids: [0, 1]`, i.e.
ρ = 0.10, constant. **As specified, H4 cannot be tested by any run in the matrix.**

Either add a ρ sweep as a fifth factor (which multiplies the run count and the cost),
or run it as a separate, smaller, dedicated experiment on one topology/population, or
drop H4 from the paper. All three are defensible; silently keeping H4 in the hypothesis
list with no experiment behind it is not.

## OQ-0005 — H(Θ) is defined but never measured
**Priority:** P0 · **Status:** OPEN · **Raised:** 2026-08-07 · **Source:** SPEC-1 §2.2 vs §3

H1 is stated as `E[I∞ | H(Θ) > τ] < E[I∞ | H(Θ) = 0]` — a claim about a continuous
diversity measure crossing a threshold τ. But the experiment manipulates a *categorical*
variable (homogeneous vs heterogeneous cohort). H(Θ) is never computed, the reference
task suite X is never specified, and τ is never given a value.

This matters in two ways. Scientifically, "heterogeneous cohorts do better" is a much
weaker and less interesting claim than "resilience increases with measured functional
diversity" — the latter is the paper's real selling point and it requires actually
measuring H(Θ) across several cohorts spanning a range of diversity. Practically, a
reviewer who sees a formal continuous hypothesis tested with a two-level categorical
will ask about it.

Also flagged for the measurement itself: pairwise JS divergence over output token
distributions is only meaningful if the models share a tokenizer and vocabulary. Across
Llama / Qwen / Gemini they do not. The measure needs redefinition — over a shared
answer space, over semantic embeddings of outputs, or over agreement/disagreement
patterns on a probe set.

## OQ-0006 — Pseudoreplication
**Priority:** P0 · **Status:** OPEN · **Raised:** 2026-08-07 · **Source:** SPEC-1 §5

Agents within a run share topology, fact, seed, and — by construction — talk to each
other. Their outcomes are strongly dependent. If the ANOVA (H1) or the K–S test (H2) is
run over agent-level observations pooled across runs, n is inflated ~20× and the
p-values are meaningless. SPEC-1 §5 says the K–S test compares "final empirical belief
distributions P(B(T))", which reads as agent-level pooling.

See SOP-030 §3 and SOP-060 §2 for the required handling.

## OQ-0007 — API call volume appears under-counted by ~3–4×
**Priority:** P0 · **Status:** OPEN · **Raised:** 2026-08-07 · **Source:** SPEC-3 §1

SPEC-3 computes `405 runs × 20 agents × 5 rounds = 40,500 total API calls`. That counts
**message generation only**. It omits belief probing entirely.

Belief must be measured per agent per timepoint. At 5 timepoints × 405 runs × 20 agents
= 40,500 probe points, and each probe point requires one logit call plus M verbal
probes (SPEC-3 §3.3 specifies M = 2 templates), the probe cost is ~121,500 additional
calls. Total ≈ **162,000 calls, not 40,500.** A baseline `G_empty` isolation pre-test
(SPEC-3 §5 item 2) adds more.

Every downstream number — free-tier feasibility, wall-clock, Modal cost — is computed
off the wrong figure. Recount before any budget claim is made.

## OQ-0008 — Capability confound
**Priority:** P1 · **Status:** OPEN · **Source:** SPEC-3 §1.1

The heterogeneous cohort (Llama-3.1-8B + Qwen-2.5-32B + Gemini-2.5-Flash + Cerebras
Llama-3-8B) has a higher average capability than the 20×Llama-3.1-8B homogeneous cohort,
and a different capability profile from the 20×70B cohort. If the heterogeneous cohort
resists misinformation better, the parsimonious explanation is "it contains stronger
models", not "diversity is protective."

The 70B "scale-matched" condition is a partial control, but matching is on *parameter
count*, not on capability, and the heterogeneous cohort's aggregate parameter count
(≈ 5×8 + 5×32 + 5×? + 5×8 B) is not matched to either homogeneous arm.

Needs, at minimum: per-model single-agent baseline accuracy on all 15 facts, reported;
and a preregistered analysis that conditions on it. Ideally a diversity manipulation
that holds mean capability fixed (e.g. several distinct cohorts of similar mean
single-agent accuracy but different composition).

## OQ-0009 — α in the dual-probe metric is unspecified
**Priority:** P1 · **Status:** OPEN · **Source:** SPEC-1 §2.4

`B_i = α·σ(...) + (1−α)·(1/M)Σ...` — α is never given a value or a derivation. It is a
free parameter sitting directly on the primary dependent variable. It must be fixed a
priori with a stated rationale, or the results reported as a sensitivity curve over α.
It may never be chosen after seeing outcomes. Interacts with OQ-0002: if the logit
component is unavailable for some agents, α is not even well-defined across conditions.

## OQ-0010 — H2's test does not test H2's claim
**Priority:** P1 · **Status:** OPEN · **Source:** SPEC-1 §3, §5

H2 claims small-world topologies produce **bimodal** belief clusters. A two-sample K–S
test detects *any* distributional difference. Significant K–S is consistent with, but
does not demonstrate, bimodality. Need a direct bimodality statistic (Hartigan's dip,
bimodality coefficient, or 1- vs 2-component mixture comparison), or restate H2 to
match the test. See SOP-060 §2.

## OQ-0011 — H3 is causal, the design is observational
**Priority:** P1 · **Status:** OPEN · **Source:** SPEC-1 §3, §5

H3 is written as a partial derivative — an intervention claim. The planned analysis
regresses observed belief change on observed message certainty. Certainty is confounded
with argument quality, message length, and the identity of the producing model. Fix by
adding a **manipulation arm**: hold argument content fixed and vary only stated
certainty via the prompt. That converts H3 from a correlation into the paper's most
defensible causal result, and it is cheap.

## OQ-0015 — Is T = 5 rounds anywhere near asymptotic?
**Priority:** P1 · **Status:** OPEN · **Source:** SPEC-1 §3 vs SPEC-3 §1

H1 is about the **asymptotic** infection rate `I∞`; H4 is about behaviour as `t → ∞`.
The protocol runs T = 5 synchronous rounds. Whether beliefs have converged by round 5 is
an empirical question that the pilot must answer. If they have not, `I∞` is not
estimable and the hypotheses must be restated over a finite horizon — which is fine, but
must be honest.

## OQ-0016 — Error placeholders contaminate the experiment
**Priority:** P1 · **Status:** OPEN · **Source:** SPEC-2 §3.4

`AsyncLLMRouter.generate_response` returns the literal string
`"[Agent Error: Latency drop]"` after three failures. That string is then broadcast into
neighbours' contexts as a message. Failure rates will differ by provider, so the
contamination is correlated with condition. See SOP-040 §6 for the required policy.

Also note the same method returns `None` implicitly if `provider` matches no branch —
a silent failure mode.

## OQ-0017 — Fact suite mixes recall with reasoning; no baseline
**Priority:** P1 · **Status:** OPEN · **Source:** SPEC-3 §2

Tier 1/2 items are factual recall. Tier 3 items (Monty Hall, bat-and-ball, lily pads,
widgets, transitivity, three-card) are **reasoning problems** where the model must
compute the answer each time, not retrieve a belief. These are different psychological
constructs and may not belong on one scale; at minimum, tier should be analysed as a
factor and not pooled.

Additionally, SPEC-3 §5 lists a `G_empty` isolation pre-test but no acceptance criterion.
Facts where a model is at floor (never gets it right alone) or ceiling (never wrong)
cannot show movement. **The fact suite must be validated against per-model baseline
accuracy and items outside a usable band excluded — before the matrix runs, and by a
preregistered rule.**

One item also needs review on its own merits: the transitivity item's stated ground
truth ("A does not strictly beat C in cyclic preference models") is a claim about
specific preference models, not a general truth, and is arguably the more confusing of
the two options presented.

## OQ-0026 — No non-communicating control arm
**Priority:** P0 · **Status:** OPEN · **Raised:** 2026-08-07 · **Source:** prior-art review B2

Confirmed 3-0 across four sources: multi-agent debate does **not** reliably outperform
single-agent baselines (Zhang et al. arXiv:2502.08788; Smit et al. arXiv:2311.17371;
Wang et al. arXiv:2402.18272, ACL 2024). We therefore cannot assume the network propagates
truth by default.

Without an isolated, non-communicating arm — each agent answers alone, no messages — the
study cannot attribute any observed outcome to the network rather than to the models'
baseline behaviour on the fact suite. SPEC-3 §5 lists a `G_empty` pre-test but treats it as
a sanity check, not as an analysed experimental condition.

**Closes with:** a DR adding `G_empty` as a first-class condition in the factorial design,
with its own cells and its own row in every results table.

## OQ-0027 — H2 is not identifiable without a truth-diffusion metric
**Priority:** P0 · **Status:** OPEN · **Raised:** 2026-08-07 · **Source:** prior-art review A4

Shen et al. (EMNLP 2025 Main, arXiv:2505.23352) **measured** that sparse topologies suppress
the propagation of *accurate* information as well as erroneous information: on questions the
system initially answers incorrectly, full connectivity raises beneficial-insight propagation
by 10.5% over the sparsest topology. Their conclusion is an interior optimum — "moderately
sparse topologies… typically achieve optimal task performance."

TRR and MP as specified measure only the error side. A finding that ER/WS graphs "resist
misinformation better than a complete graph" is therefore confounded with those graphs simply
transmitting less of everything, and a reviewer who knows this paper will say so.

**Required:** a truth-diffusion counterpart — e.g. propagation of correct belief originating
from *non-seeded* agents, or a correct-belief analogue of R_eff — reported alongside TRR/MP
for every topology.

## OQ-0028 — Graph density not matched across topologies
**Priority:** P1 · **Status:** OPEN · **Raised:** 2026-08-07 · **Source:** prior-art review A5

Planned: complete (density 1.0) vs Erdős–Rényi p=0.2 (≈0.20) vs Watts–Strogatz k=4 at N=20
(≈0.21). The complete graph differs from the other two by 5× in edge count, so any
"topology effect" involving it is confounded with connectivity.

Li et al. (arXiv:2410.13909) held density constant at 0.08 ± 0.002 across their three
topologies precisely to avoid this. It is a citable precedent that we did not.

**Options:** (a) match density across all three, dropping the complete graph or sparsifying

## OQ-0038 — Do 8B models produce graded beliefs at all?
**Priority:** P0 · **Status:** OPEN · **Raised:** 2026-08-07 · **Source:** prior-art D1 (`2604.26561`)

Sela (2026) reports that *"8B models exhibit **binary rather than graded** responses to
counter-arguments."*

The v1.0 design's primary population is 20 × Llama-3.1-**8B**, and the entire dependent
variable — `B_i^(t)` ∈ [0,1], and every metric built on it — assumes belief is *graded*. If
8B-class models flip rather than shade, then:

- belief trajectories are step functions, not curves;
- the continuous TRR is effectively a thresholded count wearing a continuous mask;
- the dual-probe's logit component may show gradation that the model's *behaviour* does not
  reflect, which is a construct-validity failure, not a feature;
- `BPI` measured as bimodality would be trivially high **for reasons unrelated to topology** —
  which would produce a spurious confirmation of H2.

That last point is the dangerous one: a measurement artefact that *manufactures* the H2
result.

**Required before G1, and cheap:** a direct gradedness check in the pilot. Probe a handful of
agents across a range of counter-argument strengths and plot the belief response curve per
model family. If it is a step, either move to a larger model class, redefine the DV as a
discrete state with survival analysis, or report gradedness per family as a first-class
finding. This is a one-day experiment that could save the project.

---

**RESOLVED 2026-08-07 — read from the full text of `2604.26561` §7.4. It is worse than the
abstract suggested, and simultaneously the best thing found in this review.**

Verbatim:

> *"8B models exhibit binary rather than graded responses to counter-arguments: they either
> maintain their position entirely (including when asked to self-reflect) or capitulate
> entirely (when exposed to opposing arguments). The absence of a 'consider and reject'
> middle state is not a universal property of language models — frontier models can engage in
> nuanced weighing of arguments — **but it appears to be a robust characteristic of the 7–9B
> parameter range**."*

DR-0005 selected exactly the 3–9B class. So the graded credence `B_i(t) ∈ [0,1]` is very
likely measuring probe noise rather than belief for most of our agents.

**But read the next paragraph:**

> *"Any multi-agent architecture that exposes small-model agents to arguments from other
> agents risks inducing **capitulation cascades**, where the first agent to encounter a
> persuasive argument flips, creating a feedback loop. Architectural designs that preserve
> agent isolation during evaluation — as our system does — are necessary safeguards at this
> model scale."*

**Sela names our phenomenon, identifies its mechanism, flags it as a hazard — and then
designs around it rather than studying it.** That is the opening. Our study measures the
capitulation cascade they deliberately avoid.

**Resolution — change the dependent variable, do not change the model class.** Move from a
graded credence to a **discrete belief state with survival analysis**: time-to-capitulation,
hazard rate, and recovery rate. This is better on four counts, not a fallback:

1. It matches the measured behaviour of the model class instead of assuming behaviour the
   class does not exhibit.
2. It fits the epidemiological framing *better* than a graded credence — SI/SIS models are
   discrete-state, and time-to-infection is exactly a survival outcome. The v1.0 design was

3. It is cheaper: a binary state needs far fewer probes than a calibrated credence, which
   directly relieves `OQ-0007`.
4. It dissolves the `OQ-0038` artefact risk: a step response would have made bimodality
   trivially high and could have *manufactured* an H2 confirmation. With a discrete DV that
   confound cannot arise.

**Still required:** verify the binary pattern in *our* models and *our* task in the pilot.
One paper, one task domain, seven models is suggestive, not settled — and if some families
are graded and others are not, gradedness-by-family becomes a reportable finding in its own
right.

**Consequential for `AMD-0001` §8 and for `DRAFT-metric-definitions.md`** — both need
revision to a discrete-state formulation.

## OQ-0043 — Debate as a martingale: a theoretical null baseline
**Priority:** P1 · **Status:** OPEN · **Raised:** 2026-08-07 · **Source:** `2604.26561` ref [13]

**Huang, Qiang, et al. "Debate or vote: LLM multi-agent debate is a martingale on belief of
the correct answer." OpenReview, 2025.** `[UNVERIFIED]`.

Sela's summary: they *"prove that multi-agent debate forms a martingale on belief in the
correct answer, providing no expected gain beyond independent voting for accuracy tasks."*

If that holds, `E[B(t+1) | B(t)] = B(t)` under normal debate — debate moves belief around but
adds nothing in expectation. **This is a gift for framing, not a threat**, for three reasons:

1. It supplies a **principled theoretical null** for the no-injection control arm. We predict
   flat expected belief there, and can test it. Very few simulation papers have a theoretical
   prediction to check their negative control against.
2. It sharpens what H1 is actually about. If the mean is a martingale, the interesting action
   is in the **variance and the tails** — cascades, capitulation, polarization — which is
   exactly what an adversary exploits and exactly what a diversity defence would damp.
3. It reframes the contribution: we are not asking "does debate help?" (answered: no, in
   expectation) but "what breaks the martingale, and what protects it?"

Combined with `OQ-0038`'s capitulation-cascade mechanism, this suggests the paper's real
story is about **cascade dynamics and tail risk**, not mean belief — which should be reflected
in the analysis plan before it is frozen.

## OQ-0047 — Weak models in heterogeneous debate: the competing explanation
**Priority:** P1 · **Status:** OPEN · **Raised:** 2026-08-07 · **Source:** `OQ-0042` residue

**Li, Yanchen, et al. "Talk isn't always cheap: Weak models in heterogeneous multi-agent
debate." arXiv:2509.05396.** `[UNVERIFIED]` — not yet obtained.

Reported to find that including weak models in heterogeneous debate can *degrade* outcomes.
This is the direct competing explanation for every published heterogeneity result: mixed
cohorts differ from homogeneous cohorts in **capability spread** as well as in diversity, and
a weak member can drag the group down regardless of how "diverse" it makes the population.

It is also the most likely mechanism behind the negative results in `2601.05606`
(*"peripheral diversity does not reliably compensate for central incompetence"*).

**Why this matters more than it looks:** our capability-matching protocol matches on *mean*
single-agent accuracy. It does not automatically match **variance**. A cohort with mean 60%
made of {40%, 60%, 80%} models is not the same object as one made of {58%, 60%, 62%}. If
Li et al. are right, the spread is doing work the mean hides.

**Required:** obtain and read; then decide whether the design matches on mean only, or on mean
**and** spread. Recommend reporting capability spread per cohort regardless — it is free, and
its absence is exactly the hole we are criticising in prior work.

## OQ-0049 — Marginal vs conditional estimand
**Priority:** P1 · **Status:** OPEN · **Raised:** 2026-08-07 · **Source:** `EXP-A01`

The validation study showed a small but systematic attenuation under run-level frailty
(bias +0.033 on a true −1.2; coverage 0.907 vs nominal 0.95). This is **expected, not a bug**:
a fixed-effects fit with cluster-robust errors estimates the **marginal** (population-averaged)
hazard ratio, while the generator's `beta_h` is the **conditional** (run-specific) coefficient.
Hazard ratios are non-collapsible, so the two are different quantities.

**Why this needs a decision rather than a footnote.** The SESOI in AMD-0002 §8.4 is currently
stated as "hazard ratio outside [0.80, 1.25]" without naming a scale. If the SESOI is
conceived conditionally and the estimate is marginal, the equivalence test in `DR-0004` compares
two different things — and a null result would be claimed against the wrong benchmark.

**Actions:**
1. State the estimand explicitly in the preregistration: *we report a marginal hazard ratio*.
2. Fix the SESOI on that same scale.
3. When `statsmodels`/`lifelines` are available, fit the frailty model as a secondary analysis
   and report both. If they disagree by more than the ~3% seen in `EXP-A01`, that is a finding
   about the data, not a technicality.

## OQ-0050 — The martingale's scope is a theoretical hook for H1
**Priority:** P1 · **Status:** OPEN · **Raised:** 2026-08-07 · **Source:** `LIT-0003`, `LIT-0004`

Choi, Zhu & Li prove multi-agent debate induces a **martingale** on belief in the correct
answer (`LIT-0003`). Zhu et al. state the scope explicitly (`LIT-0004`):

> *"under **homogeneous agents and uniform belief updates**, debate preserves expected
> correctness and therefore cannot reliably improve outcomes."*

Our design deliberately violates the first condition. That converts H1 from an empirical
hunch into a well-posed theoretical question:

> Debate among homogeneous agents preserves expected correctness. **Does architectural
> heterogeneity break the martingale — and if so, does it break it toward truth when an
> adversary is present?**

This is a sharper H1 than AMD-0001 §2 currently states, it inherits a *proved* result as its
null hypothesis, and no paper found so far answers it — because every paper studying
heterogeneity studies it cooperatively, and every paper studying adversaries uses homogeneous
populations.

**Actions before the positioning statement is final:**
1. **Read arXiv:2601.19921 in full.** If their diversity theorem generalises from
   answer-diversity to population heterogeneity, this hook weakens substantially. `[UNVERIFIED]`
   — abstract only so far.
2. Read arXiv:2508.17536's assumptions in full, and check whether our setup satisfies the
   martingale's preconditions closely enough for the no-injection prediction in AMD-0002 §5
   to be a fair test.
3. If it survives, fold into AMD-0001 §2 and the positioning statement.

**Note the constructs are separable and nobody has separated them:** their diversity is
diversity of *candidate answers at initialisation*; ours is diversity of *who is answering*,
at matched capability. A homogeneous population can be answer-diverse; an architecturally
diverse one can be answer-homogeneous.
